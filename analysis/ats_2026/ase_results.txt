Among 701,028 hospitalizations across 47 hospitals, 56,215 (8.0%) were classified as infected and 132,111 (18.8%) as ASE. The first qualifying ASE organ dysfunction occurred before first ICU admission in 49.1% and within an ICU stay’s first 48 hours in 39.0%. Compared with infected hospitalizations, positive cultures from ASE hospitalizations showed greater representation of Pseudomonas aeruginosa (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 1.30; 95% CI, 1.12–1.51), Candida glabrata (1.79; 1.54–2.07), and Stenotrophomonas maltophilia (1.70; 1.21–2.39), but lower representation of Staphylococcus aureus (0.76; 0.68–0.85) and Escherichia coli (0.77; 0.67–0.87; all q<0.05). Among 77 organism-antimicrobial pairs evaluated in adjusted models, 27 showed greater resistance in ASE (q<0.05). Standardized resistance was higher for P. aeruginosa-piperacillin/tazobactam (22.2% versus 11.7%; aOR, 2.20; 1.74–2.78), P. aeruginosa-meropenem (25.4% versus 14.3%; 2.08; 1.56–2.78), and Klebsiella pneumoniae-meropenem (6.0% versus 2.6%; 2.49; 1.75–3.54).
